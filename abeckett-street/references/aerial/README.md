# A’Beckett Street aerial and council reference notes

## How the street changes from east to west

| Section | Observed or documented features | Implication for the model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Williams Street / Ramsey Boulevard to Reilly Street | At the eastern bend, pedestrian refuges serve the junction. Shallow attached shops face A’Beckett on both sides, with parked vehicles and footpaths. Behind the north frontage is the FoodWorks car park. The 2024 aerial identifies the Esplanade Hotel on the south corner. Council’s mobility map marks a taxi rank on the south side of this block, seating along both sides and an arcade through the north frontage toward the car park. | Keep the two street fronts, their footpaths, eastern refuges and kerb parking legible from walking height. Use the historic taxi and arcade markers as checks for newer ground-level photographs. |
| Reilly Street to Alma Street | Reilly comes in from the north. On its west side the library/Hub grounds contain lawns, an open pedestrian forecourt and planted areas. Several rounded tree crowns punctuate the frontage near the Hub. A south-side run of contiguous shop roofs fronts the street toward Alma. A zebra crossing near the Hub is distinct from the eastern refuges. | Preserve the open view into the civic block. Break the street parking run for the crossing and driveways. A continuous awning or regularly spaced crowns on this north frontage would lose the identity of the block. |
| Alma Street to Sandy Mount Avenue | Alma meets from the south. In the 2022 satellite view this western segment has separate roofs, setbacks, grass, intermittent trees and driveways, with much less continuous kerbside parking. Council’s 2019 mobility map shows footpaths along both sides and grades toward the west. The 2022 aerial contains some large institutional roofs and open lawn, as well as houses. | Shift from commercial frontage and frequent kerb bays to spaced buildings, individual entries and variable crown size. Keep the footpaths continuous where the mobility map shows them. |

The 2024 council road register gives 101.1 m from the VicRoads boundary to Reilly, 107.5 m from Reilly to Alma and 152.4 m from Alma to Sandy Mount, totalling 361.0 m. The project’s OSM-derived A’Beckett centreline extends approximately 376 m between model endpoints. These lengths refer to different extents at the arterial junction. Approximate model positions are Williams/Ramsey (−38.63400, 145.72934), Reilly (about −38.63385, 145.72810), Alma (−38.63373, 145.72686) and Sandy Mount (−38.63355, 145.72506). [Bass Coast public road register, page 1](https://www.basscoast.vic.gov.au/assets/general-images/Public-Road-Register-March-2024.pdf#page=1)

## What was inspected

- A north-up overhead raster of all three blocks, a tighter view of the retail centre and a wider context were visually inspected through the public Esri World Imagery service. The imagery citation at the library returns **26 March 2022, WorldView-2, provider Vantor, 0.5 m source resolution, 5 m stated source accuracy**. Roof outlines, broad canopy, paving runs and parked vehicles are discernible. Individual species, curb ramp geometry, vehicle stalls and up-to-date shop occupancy require other sources. This is a dated image, not a claim about 2026 site conditions. The provider’s attribution record is retained in `esri-capture.json`. [Full street at satellite scale](https://services.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export?bbox=16221969.767%2C-4669501.544%2C16222577.840%2C-4669136.700&bboxSR=3857&imageSR=3857&size=870%2C522&format=jpg&f=image) · [Civic and eastern blocks](https://services.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export?bbox=16222145.788%2C-4669472.740%2C16222568.239%2C-4669178.305&bboxSR=3857&imageSR=3857&size=620%2C432&format=jpg&f=image) · [Imagery citation layer](https://services.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/4)
- The Victorian government’s public footprint layer lists two nominal 10 cm aerial projects at the same point, South Gippsland (project name dated 13 December 2021) and Outer Melbourne East (beginning 8 January 2023). The individual exposure date and image content at this street were not established from these footprint records. The separate Vicmap image server is for approved subscribers. Metadata and the exact point query are in `vicmap-footprints.json`. - The council mobility map, with information dated September 2019, was visually checked on page one. Its grey footpaths, green crossing segments, blue seat and accessible parking marks, black dashed arcades and taxi symbol locate features omitted at satellite scale. There are arcades from the northern car park and the southern shopping frontage toward Beaton Place. At the Hub the path runs between the library and the street, rounds the eastern side and meets the crossing. The 2022 council accessible-parking list also identifies a designated space in front of the Community Hub at number 16. ## Existing model

The model already contains the brick plaza, memorial on the lawn side, four mosaic blocks and the Rotary clock near the traffic lane. The local overrides explicitly preserve two-way traffic, put the clock on the north kerb extension and orient its clock faces along the street. Its `townLL` conversion places the clock near (−38.63377, 145.72797). These positions come from the project, and are more specific than the 0.5 m imagery with 5 m stated accuracy. The street-generation rule uses a nominal 20 m width and tries both sides every 26 m for trees, with clearance tests. Use observations to vary parking, canopy and ground treatment without shifting the curated landmarks.
